---
title: "Tell your story once"
description: "Save facts and five-part stories you can reuse in applications and interviews, then correct or remove them when needed."
---

You should not have to rewrite your best project from scratch for every job. Save the experience in **Career → Knowledge**, then use it when drafting answers or preparing for interviews.

Start with one useful example. You do not need to write your entire career history first.

## Add a fact

1. Open **Knowledge → Facts**.
2. Write a concrete statement in the fact input. Include your contribution and a measured result only when you know it.
3. Choose **Accomplishment**, **Experience**, **Skill** or **Preference**.
4. Select **Add**.

A useful fact names what you did: “I created a weekly review with sales and support to turn recurring customer questions into a prioritized backlog.” It is more useful than “I am a strong collaborator.”

Use **Source** on a fact to see where it came from. Facts you add yourself are your statements; the app does not independently verify them.

## Import facts from a file

Open **Knowledge → Import**, directly below Curate. Choose or drop a resume PDF, Word `.docx`, resume JSON (Reactive Resume v4, v5 or v6, or JSON Resume), or LinkedIn data export `.zip`. The same readers used by Documents turn the file into proposed facts. This flow creates Knowledge facts; use Documents when you want to create a resume.

Review **New facts** first, grouped by section or company. Edit the text and uncheck anything you don't want saved. **Duplicates** below compares proposed facts with matching facts in Knowledge or earlier in the file. Exact and possible duplicates start left out. Matching uses the same wording rules as Curate, without AI.

Comparisons stay in place while you edit. **Recheck matches** compares your current text with the latest Knowledge without discarding your edits. Select **Save facts** with the selected count when ready, or **Finish review** if nothing new needs saving.

Work history keeps each role and its dates; descriptions become accomplishments with company and role context. Existing bullets stay separate, while LinkedIn's flattened descriptions stay together. Education, certifications, languages and other sections become experience facts, and skills are combined into a list. Long text is split to fit the 2,000-character fact limit. Hidden resume entries start unchecked, and contact fields are left out.

LinkedIn ZIPs and JSON are read in your browser. Only the profile CSVs are opened from a LinkedIn archive; messages and connections are ignored. The selected facts and their original text are saved to your account after review. PDFs use your AI connection when available and the browser reader otherwise. Word files need an AI connection. Resume files can be up to 10 MB; LinkedIn archives can be up to 100 MB, with a 5 MB limit on each profile CSV. See [Importing a resume](/guides/importing-resumes) for file requirements.

New facts follow the destination group's coaching switch and privacy settings. A new group starts with coaching on. The review shows private groups and groups with coaching off. Saving doesn't run any coaching tools.

Reopening a file reads it into another review so you can compare it with Knowledge. Previously imported sources are marked **Imported before**; correct the existing fact rather than adding another copy. Saving new facts skips existing normalized text and never overwrites Knowledge. Retrying a save keeps the same source identities, so corrected, excluded, archived or forgotten facts from those sources stay as you left them.

An **Export everything** account ZIP belongs in **Settings → Your data → Import an export**. Cover-letter JSON belongs in Documents.

## Write a five-part story

Open **Knowledge → Stories → Add a story**. Give the story a clear title, add optional tags, and fill in the five parts.

| Part           | Answer this question                   | Example from a fictional project                                                     |
| -------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Situation**  | What was happening before you started? | New customers were abandoning setup.                                                 |
| **Task**       | What were you responsible for?         | Help more customers finish setup without adding support work.                        |
| **Action**     | What did you personally do?            | Interviewed customers, reviewed the funnel and worked with design on a shorter flow. |
| **Result**     | What changed, and how do you know?     | A measured improvement, if you have the records to support it.                       |
| **Reflection** | What did you learn?                    | Define success measures before starting the next rollout.                            |

Situation, Task and Action are required. Leave Result blank if you cannot support the outcome. A story can still be useful without a number. Reflection can also stay blank until you have something useful to add.

Under **Backed by**, select the facts that support the story. Then select **Save story**.

## Reuse the story

- In **Apply**, add a form question and choose **Draft** to draw on relevant Knowledge.
- In **Prepare**, open the stories suggested for the interview, or draft one that is missing.
- In **Practise**, rehearse a question using your story, then review feedback.
- In **Coach**, keep **Knowledge** on when you want those facts and stories included with your message.

Each use has a different audience. Review the wording rather than pasting the whole story into every answer. [Write an application answer](/guides/writing-application-answers) or [rehearse for an interview](/guides/preparing-for-interview-day#practise-one-answer).

## Choose what the coach remembers

At the top of **Facts**, **Remember facts from my messages** offers three choices:

| Setting           | What happens                                                                          |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Ask first**     | The default. A proposal appears below a coach answer; select **Remember** to save it. |
| **Automatically** | Facts you state can be saved, with a notice and **Undo**.                             |
| **Never**         | Facts are not saved from coach messages.                                              |

Practice answers are not remembered as career facts. You can still add a real accomplishment manually when you are ready to stand behind it.

## Correct an old fact

Select **Correct** on the fact, update the text and save it. Its earlier text remains in correction history. Stories linked to that fact are flagged when the correction is newer than the story. Switching a fact off and on again doesn't flag its stories, since its text didn't change.

Open each flagged story, check the affected claims, edit them and save the story again. Review older drafts too; correcting Knowledge does not rewrite documents or messages you already sent.

A flagged story is excluded from new coaching until you review and save it. Unfinished story text stays in this browser tab when you navigate or refresh. **Practise** opens the selected story with a relevant application; a missing story link shows an error instead of opening another story.

## Keep a detail for one job

Application notes belong to that application. A debrief can also suggest remembering a detail **for that company only**, such as what an interviewer asked you to explain next time.

Facts show their scope. Choose **Use for all applications** only when a private fact is useful beyond that job. This changes its scope, not its truth.

### Use a shared fact for some jobs only

Some facts should reach the AI only for certain jobs, such as a security clearance for government roles. Put them in a group and switch **Private** on for it in **Curate → Groups**. A private group's facts, and its subgroups', never go to your AI provider, whatever their own switches say. They stay in Knowledge, and you can still correct or forget them.

To use them for one job, open the application and switch the group on under **Knowledge for this job** in its details. Fit, Apply, Prepare, Practise, Debrief, Messages and that job's coach then read those facts, switched off or not; every other job still doesn't. Nothing is copied, so a correction to a fact applies to every job that uses it.

**Knowledge for this job** can also **Include** or **Leave out** any other group, for example to keep an unrelated past role out of one application. A choice for a single fact, made through the API (`PUT /career/overrides` with a `factId`), wins over its group's.

## Stop using or forget a fact

Switch **Used in coaching** off to keep the fact in your account while excluding it from new coaching context. A switched-off fact cannot support a story sent to the coach.

Select **Archive** to retire a fact you no longer use, such as an old skill, without erasing it. An archived fact never goes to the AI, even when its group is switched on or an application includes it, and it's hidden from the list. Choose **Archived** above the list to see archived facts, and **Restore** to bring one back, switched on.

Select **Forget** to remove the fact and its evidence links. Use **Undo** within the six-second notice if you change your mind. The original resume, file or message is not deleted, and copies already exported or sent to a provider remain outside this action. See [Your data](/guides/career-workspace-reference#your-data).

Linked story text remains yours, but its supporting links are cleared and the story stops being shared with AI. Review it and deliberately reconnect suitable facts before using it again. AI work already running with changed or forgotten Knowledge is discarded instead of publishing that obsolete result.

After you correct, switch off or forget a fact, older interview debriefs remain in **Saved** but are left out of the coach's interview history. New debriefs can use your updated Knowledge.

## Group facts and switch them together

Put related facts in a group, such as one per job or a group for anything you rarely want sent. Select **Group** on a fact and type a name, or pick one already in use. Use slashes for levels: `experience/acme` sits under `experience`. To add facts straight into a group, choose it in the **Group** filter first; new facts you add go into it.

**Career → Knowledge → Curate → Groups** lists every group with how many of its facts are switched on and off and how many characters its switched-on shared facts add to the budget. **Switch all on** and **Switch all off** change every fact in the group and its subgroups at once. A fact added to the group later starts the way the group was last switched, so a group you switched off stays off as it grows. Switching a parent group again also resets any subgroup you switched on its own.

The same controls are in the API and MCP: `GET /career/facts/groups` lists groups, `POST /career/facts/groups/status` switches one (the group goes in the body, since its path can contain slashes), `PATCH /career/facts` switches or regroups many facts by id, and `GET /career/facts?group=` lists one group's facts.

## Clean up duplicates

A fact said twice takes twice the budget. **Curate → Duplicates** finds facts with the same text, ignoring case, accents, punctuation and spacing, and facts that say nearly the same thing.

- **Remove copies** folds every word-for-word copy of a shared fact that sits in an application into the shared fact. The shared fact already reaches that application; if it's switched off or in a private group, it's included for that application instead, so nothing that application used goes missing. Saving a new copy of a shared fact to an application does the same straight away.
- **Keep one, archive the copies** keeps one of a set of identical facts, the oldest one that's switched on.
- For two facts with nearly the same words, or where the longer one says almost everything the shorter one does, choose **Keep this, archive the other** on the one you want. If you keep a fact that belongs to one application over a shared one, it becomes shared, so every application gets it, and it takes the other's group if it has none. **Not duplicates, keep both** hides the pair in this browser, for example a short and a long form you want to keep.

Keeping one archives the other: it's hidden and never sent, and you can restore it from **Archived** in Facts. Its stories, saved results and per-application choices move to the one you keep, so nothing is erased by accident.

## Fix stories the AI can't use

**Curate → Stories the AI can't use** lists each story that isn't sent and why: a fact behind it is switched off, archived, in a private group or forgotten, or was corrected after the story was saved. For a correction it shows the fact's text then and now. If the story still holds, select **Still true, use it** to confirm it as it is; otherwise **Edit story**.

## Review Knowledge with AI

**Curate → Knowledge review** checks your facts for upkeep on your default AI connection. First it removes application copies of shared facts, which needs no judgment. Then it asks the AI which facts disagree, which are older figures that a newer fact replaces (a team that grew, a count that went up), and which claim a result with no date, place or project to check it by.

It sends only your shared facts that already reach the AI: private groups, switched-off and archived facts are never sent. Each request stays within **Facts in each action** plus **Stories in each action** and the action time limit; a large Knowledge goes in up to eight requests, by group, and the summary says how many facts were read. Your provider may charge for each request.

Only judgment calls are listed, since only you can answer them. For each one choose **Archive this one** on the wrong or outdated fact, **Keep the newer one**, **Correct it**, or **Not a problem**. A finding goes away once a fact in it is archived or forgotten. **Review again** replaces the last review.

## Choose how much Knowledge goes with each request

Every AI request carries only part of your switched-on Knowledge, so the whole request fits your model, and each request has a time limit. **Settings → AI & developer → AI limits** sets both:

| Limit                                               | Default           | Used by                                                                               |
| --------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Facts shared with each message**                  | 30 facts          | Coach, sent with every message while Knowledge is on.                                 |
| **Stories shared with each message**                | 8 stories         | Coach, the same way.                                                                  |
| **Facts returned by one knowledge search**          | 12,000 characters | Coach, when it searches your Knowledge for a question.                                |
| **Stories returned by one knowledge search**        | 16,000 characters | Coach, the same way.                                                                  |
| **Facts in each action**                            | 10,000 characters | Fit, Apply, Prepare, Practise, Debrief and Messages.                                  |
| **Stories in each action**                          | 10,000 characters | The same application actions.                                                         |
| **Time limit for Fit, Apply and other actions**     | 120 seconds       | The same application actions, including scheduled preparation.                        |
| **Time limit for a whole Coach or Assistant reply** | 240 seconds       | Coach and Assistant replies, tools included.                                          |
| **Time limit for each model call in a reply**       | 120 seconds       | Each model call within a Coach or Assistant reply; never longer than the whole reply. |
| **Time limit for turning speech into text**         | 60 seconds        | Voice answers in Practise.                                                            |
| **Time limit for reading text aloud**               | 30 seconds        | Questions read aloud in Practise.                                                     |

The facts and stories that best match go in first: in application actions, the ones that match the posting and the task, such as the form question or the recruiter's message; in Coach, the ones that match your message, and the posting too when the application is shared. Matching is by shared words, counting different forms of a word as one (migrated, migration) and rare words like a tool's name more than common ones. Facts that match nothing still fill any space left, newest first, so a fact worded differently from the posting can still go in. A knowledge search finds facts and stories containing any of its words, best match first. In application actions, a story goes in only when every fact behind it does. The defaults suit fast models with small context windows; raise them when your model can read more or needs more time. Larger Knowledge limits send more of your Knowledge to your AI provider, and each request takes longer and costs more. A reply that reaches its time limit keeps what it wrote so far and asks you to continue. **Reset to defaults** puts every limit back. Time limits for web searches, page reads and scheduled discovery runs are under **Web access** on the same page.

### See how much of the budget you use

**Career → Knowledge → Curate** and **AI limits** show a **Knowledge budget** meter. It measures your shared, switched-on facts and the stories still backed by them in the same JSON characters the limits use, and shows how many the Coach shares with each message. When the facts or stories don't all fit, it says so: each action sends the best matches first and leaves out the rest.

It also estimates the tokens an action sends, at about four characters a token, counting the posting, notes and documents that go with it, and compares the largest request your limits allow with your default AI connection's context window. The window comes from [models.dev](https://models.dev). For a model it doesn't list, such as a gateway alias, select **Set it** and enter the window in tokens; it's saved on that connection. When the limits outgrow the window, the meter warns you, so you can lower them before requests get cut off.
